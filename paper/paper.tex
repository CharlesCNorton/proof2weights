\begin{abstract}
We define the forward passes of GPT-2, SmolLM2-135M-Instruct and Qwen3.5-0.8B,
whose gated DeltaNet layers carry a state matrix through the sequence, in the
Rocq prover with all arithmetic in IEEE-754 binary32 through Flocq, and extract
them to OCaml programs that run the published checkpoints. On 300 windows of
held-out text per model, 4{,}800 next-token distributions each, the extracted
forward pass and PyTorch in float32 choose the same top-1 token at every
position, with every logit within $1.6\times10^{-3}$. A second extraction that
trusts no floating-point hardware computes, at every operation and for every
input, the binary32 value the hardware extraction computes; that equality is
proved at the level of floats, so overflow, infinities, NaNs and signed zeros
are inside it, and the two assumptions the hardware extraction makes about its
host are decided on that host by a self-test. We further prove that cached
autoregressive decoding computes exactly the binary32 values
full recomputation computes, and that the three forward passes are causal. Used
as a fixed reference, the extracted pass attributes llama.cpp's divergence, top-1
changes at up to 7 of 4{,}800 positions and logit shifts up to $1.7$, to four
implementation choices; with all four removed, llama.cpp agrees as closely as
PyTorch.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A neural network deployed in float32 has no canonical output. Implementations
disagree across BLAS backends, across fused multiply-add contraction decisions
and across GPU reduction orders, and the disagreement is large enough to change
decoded tokens \cite{tinker2025,gensyn2025}. There is consequently no artifact
one can point to as the answer a given checkpoint and prompt determine.

The usual formal treatment of a network establishes a property in a proof
assistant and implements the deployed system separately, so the deployed
numbers transcribe the proven ones and the deployed arithmetic is a separate
implementation of the proven arithmetic. This work takes the other route: the
weights are read, the arithmetic is performed and the logits are produced by
functions defined in Rocq and extracted verbatim, so the arithmetic executed at
inference time is the arithmetic the proofs concern.

The contributions are the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item A binary32 definition of three architectures, including a gated DeltaNet
  recurrence, extracted to programs that run the published GPT-2,
  SmolLM2-135M-Instruct and Qwen3.5-0.8B checkpoints
  (Section~\ref{sec:exec}), measured against PyTorch and llama.cpp on 300
  held-out windows of 16 tokens per model (Section~\ref{sec:agree}).
\item Two extraction modes from one source, with a proof at the level of floats
  that they compute the same binary32 value at every operation for every input,
  overflow, infinities, NaNs and signed zeros included, and a self-test that
  decides on the host the two assumptions the hardware mode makes about it
  (Section~\ref{sec:tcb}).
\item Proofs that cached decoding equals full recomputation, as equalities of
  binary32 values, and that the three forward passes are causal
  (Section~\ref{sec:cache}).
\item A composed rounding-error bound from the primitives to the logits of all
  three architectures, a GPT-2 forward pass that computes a bound on each
  logit's distance from exact real arithmetic while it runs, and a
  backward-error statement for the linear layers (Section~\ref{sec:err}).
\item Bounds from every elementary function the models evaluate to the
  mathematical function (Section~\ref{sec:elem}).
\item A differential oracle for float32 implementations, applied to numpy on
  random models and to llama.cpp on the checkpoints (Section~\ref{sec:diff}).
\end{itemize}

The development is 19{,}919 lines of Rocq in twenty-five files and compiles
from clean in twenty-six seconds on one core, with a further one hundred and
sixty-seven seconds for the report of assumptions. Of the 193 results that
report covers, seventeen are closed under the global context, 144 rest on the
four classical axioms of the Rocq real-number library that Flocq inherits, and
32 use CoqInterval and rest in addition on the axioms of primitive floats,
63-bit integers and primitive arrays. Table~\ref{tab:assume} says which class
each result of this paper falls in.

\section{The development}
\label{sec:dev}

\subsection{Arithmetic}

The floating-point type is Flocq's \code{binary\_float} at precision $24$ and
exponent bound $128$. Addition, multiplication, division, square root,
negation, absolute value and comparison are Flocq's \code{Bplus}, \code{Bmult},
\code{Bdiv}, \code{Bsqrt}, \code{Bopp}, \code{Babs} and \code{Bcompare} under
round-to-nearest, ties-to-even. Every scalar the networks compute is produced
by one of these operations, and everything else is list plumbing that performs
no arithmetic. A weight is decoded from its four little-endian bytes through
Flocq's \code{b32\_of\_bits} composed with the single-NaN collapse, so a loaded
value is the IEEE-754 value its bytes denote.

\subsection{The elementary functions}

Each elementary function is a composition of the primitives, and each constant
is a quotient of integers that binary32 rounds once.

\paragraph{Exponential.} The argument is saturated to $[-88, 88]$ and written
as $x = k\log 2 + r$, with $k$ the nearest integer to $x/\log 2$. The reduction
subtracts $k\cdot\frac{355}{512}$ and adds $k\cdot\frac{14581891}{2^{36}}$; the
first constant has nine significant bits, so its product with every $k$ in
$[-127, 127]$ is exact, and the two together lie within $2\times10^{-12}$ of
$\log 2$. A Taylor polynomial of degree seven evaluates $e^r$ for
$|r|\le 0.35$, and the result is multiplied by $2^k$, assembled from exact
powers of two.

\paragraph{Rounding to an integer.} \code{f32\_round\_int} adds and subtracts
$c = 1.5\cdot2^{23}$. For $|y| < 2^{22}$ the sum lies in $[2^{23}, 2^{24})$,
where the unit in the last place is one, so the result is the nearest integer
for arguments of either sign. The constant has to exceed $2^{23}$: with
$c = 2^{23}$ a negative argument lands in $[2^{22}, 2^{23})$, whose unit in the
last place is one half, and \code{RoundChk.v} checks by computation that $-0.4$
and $-0.6$ then both round to $-0.5$.

\paragraph{Sine and cosine.} The argument is reduced by $k$ times a split of
$2\pi$, $\frac{201}{32} + \frac{8312081}{2^{32}}$, with $k$ the nearest integer
to $x/2\pi$, and Taylor polynomials through degree $19$ and $18$ are evaluated
on the result. The polynomial divides by the factorials as binary32 stores
them. From $14!$ upward these are not the factorials: binary32 holds
$1307674411008$ for $15!$ and $121645104594157568$ for $19!$, and the bounds of
Section~\ref{sec:elem} are stated on the stored values.

\paragraph{Logarithm and the rest.} Qwen3.5's decay term needs a logarithm,
always of an argument in $(1, 2]$, where
$\log m = 2\operatorname{artanh}\frac{m-1}{m+1}$ converges in seven terms with no
reduction. Sigmoid is $1/(1+e^{-x})$ and $\tanh x = 2\sigma(2x) - 1$. GELU is the
form GPT-2 is trained with,
$\frac{x}{2}\bigl(1 + \tanh(\sqrt{2/\pi}\,(x + 0.044715\,x^3))\bigr)$, SiLU is
$x\,\sigma(x)$, softplus is $\max(x,0) + \log(1 + e^{-|x|})$, which confines the
logarithm to $(1, 2]$, and softmax subtracts the row maximum before
exponentiating.

\subsection{Architectures}

The GPT-2 path is a pre-norm decoder stack: layer normalization, a fused
query/key/value projection, multi-head causal attention, a residual, layer
normalization, a GELU feed-forward, a residual, a final layer normalization and
a tied-embedding logit projection. Causal attention computes row $i$ by
attending the $i$-th query over the first $i + 1$ keys and values with a
max-shifted softmax, so later positions contribute no arithmetic to earlier
rows. Parameter counts are pinned by reflection;
\code{gpt2\_total\_params gpt2\_small} reduces to $124{,}439{,}808$.

The Llama path replaces layer normalization with RMSNorm, learned position
embeddings with rotary embeddings, multi-head with grouped-query attention, and
the GELU feed-forward with SwiGLU.

The Qwen3.5 path alternates three gated DeltaNet layers \cite{gateddeltanet}
with one gated full-attention layer. A DeltaNet layer projects its input to a
fused query/key/value triple, applies a depthwise causal convolution,
$\ell_2$-normalizes query and key, and runs a recurrence over a per-head state
matrix $S \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k \times d_v}$:
\[
  S_t = e^{g_t}\,S_{t-1}
        + k_t\big(v_t - S_{t-1}^{\top}k_t\big)^{\!\top}\!\beta_t,
  \qquad
  o_t = S_t^{\top} q_t ,
\]
with $g_t = -e^{A_{\log}}\operatorname{softplus}(a_t + b_{\mathrm{dt}})$ and
$\beta_t = \sigma(b_t)$. Each read-out is normalized against a gate by a
SiLU-gated RMSNorm. The full-attention layers normalize the per-head query and
key with a zero-centred RMSNorm, rotate a quarter of each head, and gate their
output elementwise by $\sigma$ of a stream carried in the query projection. For
text-only input the multimodal rotary embedding coincides with ordinary rotation
on that quarter.

The inductive extraction keeps \code{binary32} as Flocq's inductive
\code{binary\_float} and $\mathbb{Z}$ and \code{positive} as inductive
datatypes, so every float operation is the computational content of its
definition. $\mathbb{Z}$ is not mapped to machine integers, since a
mantissa-alignment shift or an intermediate product can leave their range. Only
\code{nat}, which carries indices, dimensions and token identifiers, extracts
to \code{int}. This mode trusts no floating-point hardware.

The native extraction maps \code{binary32} to the host's hardware float, in the
manner CompCert extracts its verified floats \cite{compcertfp}. Each operation
is the binary64 result rounded to binary32 through an \code{Int32} bit
round-trip.

\begin{theorem}[\code{f32\_double\_round\_plus}, \code{\_minus}, \code{\_mult}, \code{\_div}, \code{\_sqrt}]
For binary32 values $x, y$ and $\circ \in \{+,-,\times,\div\}$ (with
$y \ne 0$ for division), rounding $x \circ y$ to binary64 and then to binary32
equals rounding $x \circ y$ to binary32; likewise for $\sqrt{x}$.
\end{theorem}

The proofs instantiate Flocq's double-rounding development \cite{roux2014} at
the two formats. The width conditions hold because binary64's $53$ significand
bits exceed binary32's $24$ by the margin each operation requires, and the
exponent conditions because $e_{\min}^{64} = -1074$ lies below
$e_{\min}^{32} = -149$.

That statement is about real numbers, so it settles the two modes' agreement
only where both results are finite and it says nothing about the value either
mode returns when a result overflows, when an operand is an infinity or a NaN,
or when the result is a zero whose sign has to be chosen. \code{Narrow.v}
closes the remaining cases by moving the statement to the floats themselves. It
defines $\mathrm{widen}$, the exact embedding of a binary32 into binary64 that
occurs when an OCaml \code{float} holding a binary32 value enters an
arithmetic operation, and $\mathrm{narrow}$, the round-to-nearest-even
conversion back that the \code{Int32} bit round-trip performs, with overflow to
an infinity of the operand's sign.

\begin{theorem}[\code{narrow\_widen\_plus}, \code{\_minus}, \code{\_mult}, \code{\_div}, \code{\_sqrt}]
\label{thm:narrow}
For all binary32 values $x$ and $y$,
\[
  \mathrm{narrow}\bigl(\mathrm{widen}(x) \mathbin{\circ_{64}} \mathrm{widen}(y)\bigr)
  = x \mathbin{\circ_{32}} y
\]
for $\circ \in \{+, \times, \div\}$, with subtraction the case
$\mathrm{narrow}(\mathrm{widen}(x) +_{64} -\mathrm{widen}(y))$ because
\code{f32\_minus} is addition after negation and the native build extracts it
that way, and likewise
$\mathrm{narrow}(\sqrt{\vphantom{x}}_{64}\,\mathrm{widen}(x)) = \sqrt{\vphantom{x}}_{32}\,x$.
There is no side condition: the statement quantifies over the constructors of
\code{binary\_float}, so infinities, NaNs, signed zeros, underflow and overflow
are all inside it.
\end{theorem}

The proof is one bridge lemma applied five times. For operands on which the
operation's Flocq correctness theorem applies, the binary64 result is the
correct rounding of the same exact real value the binary32 result rounds; the
binary64 rounding never overflows, because a binary32 pair produces a quotient
of magnitude at most $2^{277}$; narrowing that result is correct rounding
again, and double rounding is harmless by the theorem above, so the two sides
have the same value, the same finiteness and the same sign, which determines a
float. The remaining operand shapes are decided by computation on the
constructors. \code{narrow\_widen} states the special case that narrowing a
widened binary32 returns it.

The consequence is that the two extraction modes compute the same binary32
value at every operation for every input, so a number measured on the native
build is a number of the inductive build. What the native build still assumes
is that the host's binary64 arithmetic is IEEE-754 round-to-nearest-even with
no excess precision and no contraction of a multiply and an add, and that
\code{Int32.bits\_of\_float} rounds to nearest. \code{runners/fp\_selftest.ml}
decides both on the host that runs it, in thirty-three checks covering excess
precision, contraction, ties to even at both widths, subnormals, overflow to an
infinity, NaN propagation and the two signed zeros; it is the first thing
\code{scripts/check.sh} runs. The elementary functions extract structurally in both
modes as the same compositions of the primitives. Table~\ref{tab:tcb} states
the two trusted bases.

\section{Execution on published checkpoints}
\label{sec:exec}

The list-based loader, which represents a file as a \code{list byte}, cannot
ingest a checkpoint of hundreds of megabytes, and the extracted transpose inside
the linear layer is quadratic in the output dimension through list indexing.
The checkpoint runners therefore read bytes natively, decode each value with the
verified \code{f32\_bytes\_to\_binary32}, and compose the verified primitives in
the order the definitions specify, streaming weights layer by layer. The GPT-2
runners decode each weight matrix, which the GPT-2 linear layer transposes,
directly in transposed order. The transposed decode is
proved equal to the transpose of the reshape (Section~\ref{sec:cache}). On a
GPT-2 fixture, the verified list-based loader followed by
\code{f32\_gpt2\_logits}, the checkpoint runner in both modes, and a
step-by-step numpy float32 reference return the same fifteen logits. The
Llama and Qwen3.5 references call \code{f32\_llama\_forward} and
\code{f32\_qwen\_forward} directly.

Table~\ref{tab:models} reports the three checkpoints against PyTorch in
float32. Each ranking agrees as deeply as the stored oracle records it, which
is through rank ten for GPT-2 and SmolLM2 and through rank eight for Qwen3.5,
whose oracle holds eight; the eighth and seventh Qwen3.5 logits are $0.021$
apart, so the depth is a property of the oracle and not of the agreement. The
largest logit difference among the top
candidates is $7.3\times10^{-5}$ absolute and $1.5\times10^{-6}$ relative, and
the greedy continuations of SmolLM2 and Qwen3.5 are token-identical over twelve
tokens. The inductive forward passes of all three models return top-ten logits
identical to the native ones at nine significant digits, which determines a
binary32 value. Timings are single-threaded wall clock on an Intel i9-12900H.
An inductive \code{binary\_float} carries its mantissa as a chain of
\code{positive} constructors, so the inductive SmolLM2 runner streams one layer
of weights at a time, and the inductive Qwen3.5 runner decodes one row of each
projection at a time.

\section{Agreement on held-out text}
\label{sec:agree}

A single prompt shows that the extracted forward pass runs a checkpoint; it does
not show how often the pass agrees with a float32 implementation. For each
model, the WikiText-2 raw test split \cite{wikitext}, tokenized by the model's
own tokenizer, was cut into consecutive blocks of 64 tokens, and the first 16
tokens of 300 blocks spread evenly over the split form the windows. Each window
was run through the native extraction, through PyTorch in float32 on an Intel
i9-13900KF and on an RTX 6000 Ada GPU, and through llama.cpp \cite{llamacpp} on
the same CPU and GPU from a float32 GGUF conversion. That conversion is
compared against the \code{safetensors} the extracted pass loads, tensor by
tensor, by \code{scripts/provenance.py}: on SmolLM2 all $272$ tensors hold the
same values, $212$ of them bit for bit and the remaining $60$, the query and
key projections of the thirty layers, after undoing the row permutation the
converter applies so that llama.cpp's rotary convention matches the
checkpoint's. The deviations reported below are therefore differences in
arithmetic and not in the weights. Each implementation returns
the logits of all 16 positions, 4{,}800 next-token distributions per model.
PyTorch runs with eager attention and with TF32 disabled, and Qwen3.5's gated
DeltaNet runs its PyTorch fallback, a chunked form of the delta rule. llama.cpp
runs with flash attention disabled and otherwise in its default configuration.
The extracted passes over the 300 windows took 1.9, 1.5 and 18 hours of CPU
time for GPT-2, SmolLM2 and Qwen3.5 on the i9-12900H.

\section{Cached decoding and causality}
\label{sec:cache}

Autoregressive generation performs a prefill over the prompt and then one
decode step per token, reusing state the earlier steps produced, while the
definitions recompute the whole sequence. The two perform the same
multiplications in different groupings, and binary32 addition is not
associative, so agreement is not automatic. Qasim et al.\ \cite{residualstream}
show that keys and values are deterministic projections of the residual stream
and verify bit-identical recomputation of them across six models from four
architecture families. The equality proved here is between a decode step that
reuses cached state and a full recomputation of the sequence, stated as an
equality of binary32 values.

\subsection{The recurrent half}

\code{f32\_delta\_scan} returns the per-token outputs of the gated delta rule,
and \code{f32\_delta\_state} is the same recursion returning the state.

\begin{theorem}[\code{delta\_scan\_app}]
For sequences split anywhere, with the five per-token streams of equal length on
the first part,
\[
  \mathrm{scan}(\beta_1\beta_2, g_1g_2, q_1q_2, k_1k_2, v_1v_2)(S)
  = \mathrm{scan}(\beta_1,\dots)(S)
    \mathbin{+\!\!+} \mathrm{scan}(\beta_2,\dots)\big(\mathrm{state}(\beta_1,\dots)(S)\big).
\]
\end{theorem}

Its corollary \code{delta\_scan\_snoc} is the decode step: extending the
sequence by one token appends exactly the output of one step from the threaded
state. \code{delta\_markov} states that the outputs after a split depend on the
prefix only through the state. \code{conv\_window\_cached\_correct} shows that
appending the current input to the trailing $k-1$ inputs forms exactly the
window \code{f32\_causal\_conv1d} reads, and \code{conv\_hist\_step} that the
runner's update maintains that invariant.

\subsection{The attention half}

\begin{theorem}[\code{causal\_attention\_app}, \code{causal\_attention\_snoc}]
If $k_1$ and $v_1$ have as many rows as $q_1$, causal attention over
$q_1q_2$, $k_1k_2$, $v_1v_2$ is causal attention over $q_1, k_1, v_1$ followed
by the rows of $q_2$, each attending over the corresponding prefix of the
extended keys and values. In particular one more position appends exactly the
new query attending over every cached key and value.
\end{theorem}

The GPT-2 runners also decode each weight matrix in transposed order rather
than calling the quadratic \code{f32\_mat\_transpose}.
\code{decode\_transposed\_correct} proves that decode equal to
\code{f32\_mat\_transpose} of the reshape, and \code{runner\_linear\_correct}
concludes that the runners' linear layer computes the values
\code{f32\_linear\_forward} computes.

\subsection{Causality}

\begin{definition}
A function $F$ from sequences to sequences is \emph{causal} when
$|F(x)| = |x|$ and $\mathrm{firstn}\,|x|\,(F(x \mathbin{+\!\!+} y)) = F(x)$ for
all $x, y$.
\end{definition}

Row-wise maps, maps that see each row's position, causal attention, the
depthwise convolution and the delta scan are causal, and causality is closed
under composition, under zipping two causal functions of the same input, and
under stacking. The multi-head regrouping of causal heads is causal, and so is
grouped-query attention when the key/value heads divide the query heads.

\begin{theorem}[\code{causal\_gpt2\_forward}, \code{causal\_llama\_forward}, \code{causal\_qwen\_forward}]
The GPT-2 forward pass is causal whenever it has a head, and the Llama and
Qwen3.5 forward passes are causal whenever each of their layers is. The Llama
and Qwen3.5 layers are causal under the head-count conditions above.
\end{theorem}

The corollaries \code{gpt2\_logits\_prefix}, \code{llama\_forward\_prefix} and
\code{qwen\_forward\_prefix} say that running a model on an extended sequence
reproduces bit for bit every row it produces for the original one. Together
with the cache theorems this is what makes a decode step that reuses cached
keys, values, recurrent state and convolution window compute the row full
recomputation computes.

\section{Rounding error}
\label{sec:err}

\subsection{The rounding model}

Write $u = 2^{-24}$ for the unit roundoff of binary32 and
$\eta = 2^{-150}$ for half its smallest positive value.

\begin{theorem}[\code{f32\_round\_mixed}]
For every real $z$ there are $d, e$ with $|d| \le u$, $|e| \le \eta$ and
$de = 0$ such that the binary32 rounding of $z$ equals $z(1+d) + e$.
\end{theorem}

This is Flocq's \code{error\_N\_FLT} at binary32. Rounding is relative in the
normal range and absolute near zero, and the statement needs no hypothesis on
$z$, so it holds at the exact zeros a network produces structurally: the
DeltaNet state starts at zero, the convolution pads with zeros, and any zero
weight zeroes a product.

\subsection{Propagation}

The relation $\mathrm{ok}\;\delta\;x\;r$ says that the float $x$ is finite and
$|x - r| \le \delta$, where $r$ is the value exact real arithmetic produces from
the same inputs. The side condition $\mathrm{regz}\;M\;z$ is
$|z|(1+u) + \eta \le M$, an overflow budget with no lower bound. An
amplification budget $L$ satisfies $L \ge 2$, $L \ge 2M$,
$L \ge 1/m + M/m^2$ and $L \ge 1/(2\sqrt{m})$, where $m$ bounds denominators and
radicands from below.

\begin{lemma}[\code{ok\_plus}, \code{ok\_mult}, \code{ok\_div}, \code{ok\_sqrt}]
If the operands are $\mathrm{ok}\;\delta$, the exact result of the operation on
the floats satisfies $\mathrm{regz}\;M$, the magnitudes the operation multiplies
are at most $M$ and the denominators and radicands at least $m$, then the
result is $\mathrm{ok}\,(uM + \eta + L\delta)$.
\end{lemma}

A stage of arithmetic depth $k$ iterates this affine map $k$ times, giving the
error $\mathrm{err}_k$, and because $\mathrm{err}_k$ is monotone in $k$ a stage
may be charged more depth than it uses. Structural lemmas transport the
relation through the list plumbing unchanged. Chaining the primitive lemmas
through the dot product, linear layers, layer normalization, the exponential,
softmax, causal attention with head splitting and concatenation, the block and
the stack gives the bound at the GPT-2 logits (\code{ok\_gpt2\_logits\_full}).
The same chain runs through RMSNorm, rotary embedding and SwiGLU to the Llama
logits (\code{ok\_llama\_logits\_full}), and through the logarithm, softplus,
the two further RMSNorm variants, the convolution, the gated delta step and
scan, and both Qwen3.5 mixers to the Qwen3.5 logits
(\code{ok\_qwen\_logits\_full}).

The side conditions of each stage are collected in a record: for the
exponential, one overflow condition per operation, bounds on the magnitudes it
multiplies, and the hypothesis that the float reduction selects the integer $k$
the real evaluation selects. \code{Witness.v} discharges the records of the
exponential, sigmoid, tanh and GELU at an exact zero, and that of the
exponential at $-1000$, which it saturates to $-88$ and whose result is
subnormal, as a masked attention score is. Every condition on a binary32
quantity is decided by computation: the function \code{Qb} reads a binary32 as
the rational it denotes, \code{Qb\_correct} proves the reading faithful, and a
condition becomes a comparison of rationals. The bounds are then instantiated
at those points (\code{exp\_at\_zero}, \code{exp\_at\_masked},
\code{sigmoid\_at\_zero}, \code{gelu\_at\_zero}). \code{Dot.v} gives the
sharper running bound for the dot product, whose per-step error is measured at
the accumulator the computation reaches (\code{f32\_dot\_error\_mixed}), and
witnesses it on a dot product containing an exact zero.

\subsection{A forward pass that carries its bound}
\label{sec:runerr}

The records above are hypotheses about intermediate values, and a checkpoint
produces billions of them. \code{RunErr.v} moves the side conditions into the
computation. The GPT-2 forward pass is written once over an abstract
arithmetic, a record of nine operations (constants, addition, negation,
multiplication, division, square root, maximum, the exponential and the dot
product), and instantiated three times. At binary32 it is
\code{f32\_gpt2\_logits}, by conversion (\code{g\_gpt2\_logits\_f32}). At exact
real arithmetic, with the exponential taken on its argument saturated to
$[-88, 88]$, it is the reference. At the annotated arithmetic of
\code{Annot.v} and \code{AnnExp.v}, every value is a binary32 result paired with
a binary64 bound.

\begin{definition}
An annotated value $(x, \delta)$ is within its bound of a real $r$, written
$\mathrm{aok}\,(x,\delta)\,r$, when finiteness of $\delta$ implies that $x$ is
finite and $|x - r| \le \delta$.
\end{definition}

Each annotated operation computes its binary32 result exactly as the extracted
primitive does. It computes the bound in binary64 with outward rounding: every
intermediate is rounded to nearest and moved to its floating-point successor or
predecessor (\code{Bound64.v}), so a finite bound lies above the real quantity
it bounds. The rounding error of the operation is read off its result as
$(u|w| + \eta)(1 + 2u)$, a consequence of \code{f32\_round\_mixed}. A condition
the analysis needs, such as a divisor that stays away from zero after its own
bound is subtracted, is tested on the values the pass computes, and a failed
test returns an infinite bound, which asserts nothing. Every annotated operation
therefore preserves $\mathrm{aok}$ with no hypothesis (\code{aok\_plus},
\code{aok\_mult}, \code{aok\_div}, \code{aok\_sqrt}, \code{aok\_max}).

The exponential needs no agreement between the float and the real reduction.
Whatever integer $n$ the float reduction selects,
$e^{x} = 2^{n} e^{x - n\log 2}$, so \code{ann\_exp} tests that the selected
value is an integer of magnitude at most $127$ and that the reduced argument,
widened by its bound, lies within $179/512$ of zero, where the series bound of
Section~\ref{sec:elem} applies. The distance from the exponential of the
argument to that of the real argument is charged relative to the result,
through an upper bound on $e^{\delta} - 1$ computed by halving and squaring
(\code{aok\_exp}).

\begin{theorem}[\code{gpt2\_logits\_bounded}]
For every configuration, model and token sequence, the binary32 components of
the annotated forward pass are the logits \code{f32\_gpt2\_logits} returns, and
each is $\mathrm{aok}$ of the corresponding logit of the reference network on
the same weights and tokens.
\end{theorem}

One relational theorem, stated for any relation the nine operations preserve,
gives both halves. The bound is computed by the pass from the values that pass
produces, so it holds for the input the pass was run on.
\code{runners/gpt2\_bound\_ref} runs the inductive extraction of this function on
models loaded through the verified loader, and \code{runners/gpt2\_bound\_native}
runs its native extraction on the checkpoint.

\subsection{Backward error}

\begin{theorem}[\code{f32\_dot\_backward}]
If every multiply-accumulate step of $\mathrm{dot}(x, y)$ of length $n$ stays
where rounding is relative, the computed value equals
$\sum_i x_i y_i t_i$ for factors with $|t_i - 1| \le (1+u)^{n+1} - 1$.
\end{theorem}

The perturbation is relative and set by the length of the one dot product, not
by the depth of the network. The statement lifts to the matrix-vector product
and to the tied-embedding projection of all three models
(\code{f32\_mat\_vec\_mul\_backward}, \code{logits\_backward}), where the longest
dot products are \cite{higham2002}.

\section{The elementary functions against mathematics}
\label{sec:elem}

The propagation relation compares a float evaluation with the real evaluation
of the same expression. This section bounds the distance from the real
evaluation to the mathematical function, and the composition by the triangle
inequality gives the distance from the extracted function to the mathematical
one.

\subsection{Series}

\code{Series.v} states, and CoqInterval \cite{coqinterval} proves, the
truncation error of each polynomial with the divisors the code holds: the sine
polynomial within $2\times10^{-9}$ of $\sin$ and the cosine polynomial within
$2\times10^{-8}$ of $\cos$ on $[-3.17, 3.17]$, the exponential polynomial within
$10^{-8}$ of $\exp$ for $|r| \le 0.35$, and the arctanh series within
$2\times10^{-8}$ of $\log$ on $[1, 2.0001]$, together with bounds on the stored
constants. The file imports only the reals and the interval tactic, which keeps
each interval proof below a second.

\subsection{Exponential}

\begin{theorem}[\code{exp\_core\_vs\_true}]
For $-88 \le x \le 88$ the real evaluation $E(x)$ of the reduced exponential
satisfies $|E(x) - e^x| \le 1.6\times10^{-8}\, e^x$.
\end{theorem}

The reduced argument satisfies $|r| \le 0.35$. The polynomial is within
$10^{-8}$ of $e^r$, the stored split of $\log 2$ moves the exponent by at most
$127 \cdot 2\times10^{-12}$, which changes the exponential by a relative
$4\times10^{-10}$, and multiplying by $2^k$ scales both errors by the same
factor as $e^x$. \code{ok\_exp\_true} composes this with the propagation bound;
the relative form is what the scaling makes possible, since no step amplifies
an incoming relative error.

\subsection{Sine and cosine}

\begin{theorem}[\code{reduce\_vs\_true}]
For $|x| \le 262144$ the real reduction $\rho(x) = x - k\,(201/32) - k\,(8312081/2^{32})$
satisfies $|\rho(x)| \le 3.16$, $|\sin\rho(x) - \sin x| \le 8.4\times10^{-7}$ and
$|\cos\rho(x) - \cos x| \le 8.4\times10^{-7}$.
\end{theorem}

The range is the largest rotary angle any of the three checkpoints forms. An
angle is a position times an inverse frequency, the largest inverse frequency
is one, so the largest angle is the context length, and Qwen3.5's $262144$ is
the longest. The integer $k$ is at most $41722$ in magnitude, the stored split
differs from $2\pi$ by at most $2\times10^{-11}$, and the stored $1/2\pi$
differs from $1/2\pi$ by at most $10^{-8}$, which keeps $x - 2\pi k$ within
$3.159$ of zero. Both quantities grow with the range: the reduction is charged
the split's error once per multiple of $2\pi$ removed, which is what takes the
bound from $1.3\times10^{-8}$ at $|x| \le 4000$ to $8.4\times10^{-7}$ here, and
the interval the reduced argument occupies widens with the error in the stored
$1/2\pi$, which is why \code{Series.v} states its truncation bounds on
$[-3.17, 3.17]$ rather than on $[-\pi, \pi]$. The integer the reduction selects
converts to binary32 exactly for every $|k| < 2^{24}$
(\code{f32\_of\_Z\_exact}), so it is the series interval and not the conversion
that fixes the range.
Periodicity over the integers and the Lipschitz bounds
$|\sin p - \sin q| \le |p - q|$ and $|\cos p - \cos q| \le |p - q|$, both derived
from the product formulas, finish the proof. \code{ok\_reduce\_2pi} carries the
propagation relation through the reduction under the same kind of hypothesis
as the exponential's, that the float and real evaluations select the same $k$.

The side conditions of a stage are collected in a record, and the reduction's
is the smallest one worth displaying in full. Writing $k$ for the integer the
real evaluation selects and $\hat k$ for \code{f32\_of\_Z} of it,
$\mathrm{red\_reg}\;M\;m\;x\;r$ is the conjunction of eight clauses: that the
float reduction's rounded product agrees with $\hat k$ bit for bit; that
$|\hat k|$, $|{\cdot}\,\mathrm{hi}|$ and $|{\cdot}\,\mathrm{lo}|$ are each at
most $M$; that the two products $\hat k \cdot \mathrm{hi}$ and
$\hat k \cdot \mathrm{lo}$ satisfy $\mathrm{regz}\;M$; and that the two
subtractions do as well. The first clause is the one that is a genuine
hypothesis rather than a magnitude bound, and it is the assumption the
measurement of Section~\ref{sec:diff} exercises without discharging: it says
that the float reduction lands on the integer the real reduction lands on. The
records for the other stages have the same shape, one overflow clause per
operation and one magnitude bound per factor, and are discharged pointwise in
\code{Witness.v}.

\begin{corollary}[\code{ok\_sin\_true\_full}, \code{ok\_cos\_true\_full}]
Under the reduction's and the polynomial's side conditions, for
$|x| \le 262144$ the extracted \code{f32\_sin} is within
$\mathrm{err}_{n+23} + 8.5\times10^{-7}$ of $\sin x$ and \code{f32\_cos} within
$\mathrm{err}_{n+22} + 8.7\times10^{-7}$ of $\cos x$, where the input is
$\mathrm{ok}\;\mathrm{err}_n$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The compositions}

Sigmoid follows from the exponential by the inequality
$|1/(1+a) - 1/(1+b)| \le \delta$ whenever $|a - b| \le b\delta$ and
$\delta \le 1$, which gives $|\sigma_{\mathrm{real}} - \sigma| \le 1.6\times10^{-8}$
on $[-88, 88]$. The identity $\tanh y = 2\sigma(2y) - 1$ doubles it. Softplus
uses $\max(x,0) + \log(1 + e^{-|x|}) = \log(1 + e^x)$ and
$|\log p - \log q| \le |p - q|$ for $p, q \ge 1$, derived from
$1 + t \le e^t$. GELU additionally needs $|\tanh s - \tanh t| \le 8|s - t|$ for
$|s - t| \le 1/16$, derived from the sigmoid form and
$|e^w - 1| \le 2|w|$ for $|w| \le 1/4$, and the stored constants
$6693141/2^{23}$ and $12003091/2^{28}$, within $2.3\times10^{-8}$ of
$\sqrt{2/\pi}$ and $1.6\times10^{-9}$ of $0.044715$. Table~\ref{tab:elem} lists
the resulting bounds beside the errors measured on the inductive extraction
(Section~\ref{sec:diff}). The measured errors include the rounding the proved
bounds leave to the propagation term.

\section{Differential testing}
\label{sec:diff}

Because the extracted forward pass fixes every rounding and every reduction
order, it is a fixed reference against which other float32 implementations can
be measured.

\subsection{On random models}

The harness builds random small models, runs each through the
inductive extraction and through a numpy float32 implementation of the same
elementwise operations with numpy's own reductions, and reports the divergence
and any final-position next-token disagreement. Weights cross as raw binary32
bit patterns, so the two sides start from identical values and differ only in
reduction order. The GPT-2 reference goes through the verified list-based
loader; the Llama and Qwen3.5 references call the Rocq-level forward passes.

Across $368$ models, sixteen per configuration, no configuration produces a
next-token disagreement (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}). Divergence tracks width: moving
$d_{\mathrm{model}}$ from $8$ to $64$ at four layers raises the mean absolute
logit error by a factor of $12.5$, while moving from one layer to eight raises
it by $1.4$, and sequence length and vocabulary size have no discernible effect.
That is what accumulating longer dot products in a different order predicts,
and it is consistent with probabilistic analyses of rounding-error growth
\cite{highammary2019}. The Qwen3.5 rows sit about an order of magnitude above
the others, which is what a recurrence carrying a state matrix across the
sequence, on top of a logarithm and a convolution, predicts.

The architecture sweep supplies the rotary tables as data, so it does not
evaluate \code{f32\_sin} or \code{f32\_cos}. A separate driver evaluates every
elementary function on the inductive extraction at $40{,}001$ inputs, passed
and returned as binary32 bit patterns, and compares the results with the
mathematical function in double precision. It reports the maximum error, in
Table~\ref{tab:elem}, and the number of inputs whose sign disagrees with the
mathematical function, which exposes a reduction landing on the wrong multiple
of the period; that number is zero for every function, and stays zero over the
whole rotary range, where the reduction removes up to $41{,}722$ multiples of
$2\pi$.

\subsection{At checkpoint scale}

The same reference applies to the checkpoints of Section~\ref{sec:agree}. There
PyTorch never changes a top-1 token and stays within $1.57\times10^{-3}$ of every
logit of the extracted forward pass, while llama.cpp in its default
configuration changes the top-1 token at up to 7 of 4{,}800 positions and moves
logits by up to $1.70$. Because the reference fixes every rounding, such a
deviation can be attributed by changing one implementation choice at a time.
Four choices in llama.cpp are involved. It stores the key/value cache in float16 by default. Its
CPU backend evaluates GELU through a table of 65{,}536 float16 entries indexed by
the float16 rounding of the argument. Its CUDA backend sets cuBLAS to TF32 math,
NVIDIA's reduced-precision mode for float32 matrix products on recent GPUs, and
when a batch holds at most 16 tokens it multiplies float32 weight matrices of
suitable shape in its own kernel, \code{mul\_mat\_f}, built on the GPU's
tensor-core matrix instructions, which the cuBLAS math mode does not govern.
Table~\ref{tab:llamacpp} runs every combination: a float32 cache through the
context parameters, TF32 disabled through \code{NVIDIA\_TF32\_OVERRIDE=0}, and
the table and the kernel removed by deleting \code{GGML\_GELU\_FP16} from the
CPU backend and the \code{mul\_mat\_f} branch from \code{ggml\_cuda\_mul\_mat}.

\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Agreement with PyTorch is measured.} There is no independent
specification of what these models compute in floating point against which the
development could be proved correct; the development is itself such a
specification.

\paragraph{The checkpoint runners are partly ordinary OCaml.} The transposed
decode and the caches, which regroup the arithmetic, are proved to compute the
values the definitions compute. The rest of the runners' composition, file
input and the order of layers, is checked by the fixture comparison and by the
inductive references rather than proved. This is the widest gap between what is
proved and what is measured, and it is the reason the agreement of
Section~\ref{sec:agree} is evidence and not a theorem. Closing it means defining
the streaming, layer-by-layer pass in Rocq over an abstract weight source,
proving it equal to \code{f32\_gpt2\_logits}, \code{f32\_llama\_forward} and
\code{f32\_qwen\_forward}, and reducing the runners to byte input and output;
the equalities of Section~\ref{sec:cache} are the fragments of that proof that
exist. Theorem~\ref{thm:narrow} does not reach it: that theorem is about the
arithmetic, and this is about the composition around it.

\paragraph{The error bounds are worst-case.} The composed bound holds under its
side-condition records, which are hypotheses about the inputs, discharged for
the elementwise stages at the points of Section~\ref{sec:err}. The bound the
annotated forward pass computes needs no hypothesis and holds for the input it
was computed on, but it propagates intervals: it lies within three orders of
magnitude of the observed error on one-layer models and is infinite on GPT-2
small from the first feed-forward block on. It is proved for the GPT-2 path. The
bounds against the mathematical functions carry the hypotheses of the composed
bound, and the one on GELU uses a Lipschitz constant rather than the saturation
of $\tanh$.

\paragraph{The receipt is not a security mechanism and is claimed as none.}
\code{Receipt.v} defines a record binding a weight checksum, the prompt and the
output, and proves it is checked by recomputation
(\code{verify\_receipt\_sound}) and that its polynomial rolling hash modulo
$2^{32}$ changes whenever a single byte of the file changes
(\code{checksum\_detects\_single\_byte}). Those are the only claims made for
it. It detects accident, not tampering: the hash is not collision resistant,
and no threat model is offered under which it would be.

\paragraph{The inductive mode is slow.} Bignum arithmetic runs at microseconds
per operation, which puts a checkpoint forward pass at an hour to several hours.
Theorem~\ref{thm:narrow} is what keeps that from limiting the claims: the two
modes agree at every operation by proof, so the hardware mode's numbers are the
inductive mode's numbers and nothing is established by running the slow one at
scale.

\section{Related work}

Flocq \cite{flocq} provides the IEEE-754 formalization this development computes
in; CompCert's verified floating point \cite{compcertfp} is the model for
extracting Flocq arithmetic to an executable; the double-rounding results
instantiate Roux's development \cite{roux2014}; and Coq's primitive floats
\cite{primfloats} are an alternative route to native speed with a different
trusted base. CoqInterval \cite{coqinterval} discharges the series bounds.

TorchLean \cite{torchlean} is a framework for formalizing, executing and
verifying neural networks in Lean~4. It provides a PyTorch-style API with exact
and finite-precision tensor semantics, executable IEEE-style binary32
components with a rounding model, verified reverse-mode differentiation,
interval and affine bound propagation with CROWN/LiRPA-style certificate
checking, and semantic layers for attention, FlashAttention and state-space
models. The development here has a narrower operator set and a different
object: definitions extracted and executed on published checkpoints, with
results about that execution. Earlier proof-assistant treatments of neural
networks work at smaller scale: Certigrad \cite{certigrad} verifies a stochastic computation
graph over the reals, MLCert \cite{mlcert} extracts quantized networks, and
Brucker and Stell \cite{brucker} handle feedforward classifiers in
Isabelle/HOL. Jia and Rinard \cite{jiarinard} show that floating-point error in
network verifiers is exploitable.

For mechanized error analysis the closest work is LAProof \cite{laproof}, which
proves forward and mixed backward bounds for dot products and matrix-vector
products with underflow handled; the rounding model of Section~\ref{sec:err} is
the same device. VCFloat \cite{vcfloat} treats approximation as well as
rounding error. The dot-product backward-error statement is the classical form
\cite{higham2002}. For transformers, Budzinskiy et al.\ \cite{budzinskiy} give a
mixed-precision stability analysis covering normalization layers, softmax and
self-attention.

Batch-invariant kernels \cite{tinker2025} and RepOps \cite{gensyn2025} address
nondeterminism by fixing the reduction order in the implementation rather than
specifying it in a proof assistant. Three further lines take a reference
without building one in a prover. Cankaya \cite{bitexact} shows that vLLM and
Hugging Face transformers produce deterministic but non-invariant outputs and
that bit-exact recomputation is possible across NVIDIA GPU variants by software
emulation, so accumulated rounding becomes a signature of the setup that
produced an output; the reference there is a rerun of the same software, where
the reference here is a definition. Srivastava, Arora and Boneh \cite{optver}
control hardware nondeterminism to make a training run auditable by a verifier
who reruns it. Somani \cite{somani} compiles circuit explanations into bounded
propositions an SMT solver decides over finite symbolic domains, which needs an
operator stack that is piecewise linear and therefore replaces softmax; the
development here keeps the operators the checkpoints were trained with and
gives up decidability for them. George \cite{torchleancheck} reports a Lean
layer over TorchLean that checks exported checkpoint files, traces and
summaries against the claim they support, which is the same concern as
Section~\ref{sec:exec} at the level of files rather than of arithmetic. Correctly rounded binary32 elementary
functions \cite{rlibm32,coremath} are an alternative to the series used here,
which would make the reference canonical against mathematics at the cost of
moving it away from PyTorch, whose backends are not correctly rounded. Gated
DeltaNet is due to Yang, Kautz and Hatamizadeh \cite{gateddeltanet}.

\section{What each result rests on}
\label{sec:assume}

\code{theories/Audit.v} reports the assumptions behind every result named in
this paper. Table~\ref{tab:assume} gives the class of each, generated from that
report by \code{scripts/assumption\_table.py} rather than transcribed. Three
classes occur. A result closed under the global context uses no axiom at all. A
classical result rests on the four axioms of the Rocq real-number library that
Flocq inherits, which is where every statement about real numbers necessarily
lands. The results that call CoqInterval rest in addition on its
primitive-float, 63-bit-integer and primitive-array interface, and those are
the bounds against the mathematical functions: the series truncations of
\code{Series.v} and everything composed from them. No result of this paper is
closed under the global context and also about floating-point values, because
the value of a float is a real number.

\section*{Availability}

The development, the runners, the setup and harness scripts, and the aggregate
results behind Tables~\ref{tab:bound}, \ref{tab:agree}, \ref{tab:elem},
\ref{tab:sweep} and~\ref{tab:llamacpp} are at
\url{https://github.com/CharlesCNorton/proof2weights}. The theories compile
under Rocq~9.0 with \code{coq-flocq} and \code{coq-interval}, and
\code{theories/Audit.v} reports the assumptions behind the results.

\code{scripts/check.sh} runs the checks and prints the time each takes: the
theories, the assumption report, the self-test of the host the native build
runs on, the provenance record, the elementary functions against the
mathematical ones, the three checkpoints against their PyTorch oracles, and the
bound the annotated pass carries. A step whose input is absent is reported as
skipped and named, so the output says what was decided and what was not. On a
machine with the runners and checkpoints present it takes nine minutes, and a
further three to compile the theories and the assumption report.

\code{scripts/provenance.py} writes the record that says which bytes every
number here was produced from: the Hugging Face commit each checkpoint was read
at, the digest of the converted \code{safetensors} the runners load, and the
version of Rocq, Flocq, CoqInterval, PyTorch, CUDA, transformers and llama.cpp.
The commits are \code{607a30d7} for GPT-2, \code{12fd25f7} for
SmolLM2-135M-Instruct and \code{2fc06364} for Qwen3.5-0.8B.
Checkpoint revisions move, so a rerun that reports a different commit is
measuring different weights. The per-window logit dumps behind
Table~\ref{tab:agree} are regenerated by \code{scripts/agree\_setup.py} and
\code{scripts/agree\_torch.py} and are not in the repository; the windows they
build are determined by the split, the tokenizer and the three integers the
first of those scripts takes, so they are the same windows on any machine.
